Admissions calls follow a fixed six-step insurance verification sequence.

D109
ProcessSOP
  • 1. Confirm patient name, date of birth, and member ID; if no member ID, collect their Social Security number to pull it.
  • 2. If the client is in a grace period, either collect a payment or confirm there is an active PE (presumptive eligibility) via the portal before moving forward.
  • 3. If the primary insurance isn't accepted but the secondary is, the patient can be taken on the secondary coverage.
  • 4. For any commercial PPO plan not otherwise listed as in network, check for out-of-state and out-of-network benefits before assuming the patient cannot be admitted.
  • 5. For Medicare with Medicaid as secondary, document both policies and message Billing on Teams (see the dual Medicare/Medicaid rule).
  • 6. If uncertain on any plan, place the caller on hold and verify with a supervisor before committing.

This protocol applies only when a call moves past general distress into genuine concern for immediate safety of the caller or someone else.

D056
PolicySOP
It is not for every hard or emotional call. Substance use changes how risk shows up: intoxication or withdrawal can look erratic, aggressive, or confused without being a safety crisis, and can also mask real warning signs. Quick-reference crisis resources and exactly when to use each one.

D057
ReferenceSOP
  • 911 — immediate, life-threatening danger; an active attempt; a weapon or method in hand; an active overdose; or you cannot keep the caller safe on the phone.
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text) — caller is thinking about suicide but not in immediate physical danger, or doesn't want 911 involved. 24/7, connects to a local counselor.
  • Crisis Text Line — text HOME to 741741, for callers/contacts more comfortable texting.
  • Parkview Behavioral Health Institute (PBHI) HelpLine — 260-471-9440 or 1-800-284-8439, Fort Wayne/Allen County 24/7 crisis line, can dispatch a mobile crisis (SOS) team to the caller's location if they don't feel safe with 911.

Warning signs to listen for on a call, and the exact signs of an active overdose.

D058
ReferenceSOP
Suicide/self-harm warning signs: talk of wanting to die or not waking up, self-hatred or feeling like a burden, talk of hurting/harming self even vaguely, sudden hopelessness or a sudden unexplained calm after distress (can signal a decision has been made), giving things away/saying goodbye/getting affairs in order, expressing rage or threats toward a specific person.

Overdose signs: unresponsive or can't be woken, slow/shallow/stopped breathing, gurgling or choking sounds, blue or gray lips/fingertips, pinpoint pupils.

Assess suicide/self-harm risk directly using a specific set of plain-language questions.

D060
ProcessSOP
Stay calm, slow your pace, and ask directly:
  • "Are you thinking about hurting yourself or ending your life right now?"
  • "Do you have a plan for how you'd do it?"
  • "Do you have access to what you'd use?"
  • "Are you cutting, burning, or hitting yourself right now?"
  • "Are you alone right now, or is someone with you?"
  • "Where are you located right now?" — get the exact address if at all possible.

Be transparent with the caller when involving 911, and only override their wishes when danger is immediate.

D063
ProcessSOP
Tell them what you're doing and why ("I'm concerned about your safety right now... are you all right with me calling 911 and staying on the line with you the whole time?"), and offer a three-way call so they aren't alone. If they're resistant but not in immediate danger, offer lower-barrier options first (988 or PBHI's SOS team) instead of police. If there is immediate danger — an active attempt, a weapon/method in hand, or the caller goes silent or hangs up — call 911 regardless of what they want; safety comes first. After the call, stay honest and warm ("I called because I care about you"), don't apologize for keeping them safe, and stay present.

Never hang up first or put a crisis caller on hold, and avoid specific unhelpful phrases.

D066
StandardSOP
Keep your voice calm and steady, slow your own pace to help regulate theirs, and reassure them repeatedly out loud ("I'm not going anywhere. I'm here with you."). Do not minimize what they're feeling (avoid "it's not that bad" or "you don't really mean that"); do not promise secrecy, since supervisors or emergency services may need to be involved; and do not rush to end the call the moment you have the information you need — stay present with them.
